## Executive Summary

W47 was a selective rotation into scalable AI compute, fintech distribution, defense autonomy and genomic tooling—not a clean, market-wide risk-on signal. The current fact base records 25 official notification rows on Nov. 9 and Nov. 13: 10 buys and 15 sells. The strongest cross-fund action was NVDA bought by ARKF, ARKK, ARKQ, ARKW and ARKX. ARKF and ARKK also bought SOFI; ARKG added SCTX; ARKQ added KTOS. The visible funding or cleanup sleeve included TEM, BYDDY, BLSH, TER, AVGO, XYZ, PLTR, SNPS, ARKB, HONA and PSNL.

The KAIROS read is a receipt filter: capital is moving toward businesses whose next proof can be named—compute capacity, members and revenue, defense contracts and production, or genomic tests and platform adoption. The file is unreconciled and incomplete, so execution dollars, complete weekly net flow and ARK motive remain unknown.

## Evidence By Issue: Top Three Kairos Moments

### 1. Cross-fund NVDA buying is a scale bet, not a blanket AI verdict

**Fact — official trade file:** On Nov. 9, all five ARK equity funds in the fact base—ARKF, ARKK, ARKQ, ARKW and ARKX—reported NVDA buys. The same 21-row file included SOFI buys in ARKF and ARKK, while AVGO, TER, TEM, XYZ, ARKB, BLSH and other names were sold. This is cross-fund concentration in one compute-scale name alongside selective funding trades; it does not establish motive, valuation or a portfolio-wide increase in AI risk. [ARK trade file](https://etfs.ark-funds.com/hubfs/idt/trades/ARK_Trades.xls)

**Operating anchor:** The existing KAIROS moment ties the compute signal to the physical bottleneck of power, cooling, transmission and site capacity. The relevant test is not another model announcement but whether accelerator demand converts into delivered capacity, utilization, margin and cash flow. [KAIROS Compute to Power Bottleneck](https://kairos.ju2189ts.workers.dev/)

**Inference / KAIROS view:** NVDA is being used as the scalable leg of the basket while AVGO, TER and CRWV provide funding-side contrast. The falsifier is persistent buying without capacity, customer, margin or cash-conversion evidence—or a quick reversal across the same funds.

## Capital Movement And Coverage

- **Weekly repository coverage:** 25 official notification rows from Nov. 9 and Nov. 13: 10 buys and 15 sells. Nov. 10–12 and Nov. 14–15 have no verified rows in the current fact base; that is a coverage gap, not evidence of no trading.
- **Repeated buy clusters:** NVDA appeared five times, SOFI twice and SCTX twice. KTOS was added on Nov. 13 after its Nov. 6 cross-fund buys in the prior week. The most concentrated visible add was NVDA across five funds.
- **Funding / substitution:** TEM, BYDDY, BLSH and TER each appeared twice on the sell side. AVGO, XYZ, PLTR, SNPS, ARKB, HONA and PSNL were also sold. These are funding-side observations, not motive or rejection evidence.
- **Coverage limits:** ARK states that trade notifications are unofficial, unreconciled, incomplete and may exclude activity. Share counts and ETF weights cannot establish execution dollars, complete weekly net flow, market-wide consensus or motive.

The capital map therefore reads as selective receipt-seeking reallocation. Whether it becomes durable depends on capacity, contracts, members, tests and cash conversion—not on the number of tickers in the tape.

## Risk Analysis

- **Manager scope:** ARK is one manager; the file cannot establish institutional consensus.
- **Causality:** Company results and trade notifications are adjacent evidence, not proof that one caused the other.
- **Execution:** AI compute, defense, genomics and fintech each have different bottlenecks: power and utilization, certification and production, test adoption and reimbursement, or credit quality and rate sensitivity.
- **Valuation and dilution:** Strong operating receipts do not remove multiple, issuance, cash-burn or concentration risk.
- **Data:** Only two trading dates are represented for W47; the rolling file does not provide reconciled dollar net flow.
